# Round 14 Madrid voice-over

Round 14 took us to Madrid, and Formula 1’s first race at the new Madring delivered a strategy swing that changed everything.

Lando Norris started from pole and looked in control, building a gap at the front. But when Lance Stroll stopped and triggered a Virtual Safety Car, Mercedes and Red Bull got the cheap stop while Norris missed the window. A slow seven-second pit stop made things worse, dropping him behind Kimi Antonelli and Max Verstappen.

Antonelli held on to take his eighth win of the season, Verstappen finished second and Norris recovered to third.

Now to the SAVI League, where Louise Mihalev has done it again.

We Are the Competition wins Madrid with 185 points, giving Louise her third victory of the season and her fifth podium overall. That now puts her level with Max Cutter for both wins and total podiums this season.

Phil Motteram delivers a huge result for Blue Flag Racing, finishing second on 181. It is his second silver of the year, and after finishing fifth in Italy he has now put together back-to-back top-five results. Even better, Phil has climbed four championship places across the last two rounds, from fifteenth before Monza to eleventh after Madrid.

Championship leader Max Cutter takes third with 176. It is Max’s first bronze, his fifth podium of the year, and another weekend where he has taken points out of JR10044.

Then comes Fooch. Fourth again.

That is now fourth place in Great Britain, Belgium, the Netherlands and Madrid. Add the shared second place in Italy, and Box Box Box has finished inside the top four in five of the last six rounds. Fooch has only one podium all season, but that consistency has been enough to keep third place in the championship.

JR10044 finishes fifth in Madrid on 159. He is still second overall, but has now gone five consecutive races without a podium since winning Great Britain.

And that brings us to the biggest championship story at the front.

JR arrived in the Netherlands with a 24-point lead over Max. Across the Netherlands, Italy and Madrid, Max has outscored him by 78 points. Three races later, Max leads by 54.

Behind the leaders, Madrid has turned the midfield into a traffic jam.

Richard Saing climbs two places to seventh overall. Simon Rogerson, last round’s winner in Italy, scores 141 in Madrid but still holds eighth. Adrian Jenkins moves up to ninth, Matt Magraith drops three places to tenth, and Phil Motteram is now eleventh.

Here is how tight that group is: only 10 points separate seventh from eleventh, and just four points cover Simon in eighth, Adrian in ninth, Matt in tenth and Phil in eleventh. One strong round could completely reorder that section of the championship.

Martin West is quietly building momentum as well. He gained three championship places in Italy and another two in Madrid. That is five positions gained across two rounds.

At the other end of that trend, Jon Evans has gone the opposite way. He dropped five positions in Italy and another one in Madrid, a six-place slide across the last two races.

Back near the front, Louise’s win moves her from fifth to fourth overall. She was sixth after the Netherlands, fifth after Italy, and now fourth after Madrid.

She also takes 19 points out of Fooch this round, cutting the gap to third from 43 points to just 24. Peter Raimondo drops to fifth, only 22 points behind Louise, meaning just 46 points cover third through fifth.

Fourteen rounds complete. Max has finally built some breathing room, Louise is charging towards the championship podium, Fooch keeps finding fourth place, and the midfield could reshuffle completely in one weekend.

Next stop, Baku.
