# SAVVY F1 League | Round 16 Bahrain voice-over

Round 16 took Formula 1 back to Sepang for the Bahrain Grand Prix in Malaysia, and the weather turned the race upside down. Heavy rain delayed the start, the field split between intermediates and slicks, and Max Verstappen had to recover after both Mercedes cars beat him into Turn One. Verstappen worked his way back to the front for his first win of the season, Kimi Antonelli finished second, and Lewis Hamilton recovered from an early tyre gamble to take third. George Russell retired late, leaving Antonelli 84 points clear in the Formula 1 championship.

Now to the SAVVY League, where Louise Mihalev is no longer just charging towards the title. She is right in the middle of it. We Are the Competition wins Bahrain with 210 points, completing three victories in a row and five wins in the last seven rounds. Louise finished sixth last year, but she now shares second place this season, only 52 points behind Max Cutter.

Goodie takes second for Ferrari strategy department with 166 points, the team's first podium since winning Monaco. JR10044 finishes third on 165, ending a six-race podium drought stretching back to Great Britain.

The championship picture has changed sharply. Louise entered Bahrain 45 points behind JR and outscored JR by exactly 45, leaving them tied on 2,951. She also took 69 points out of Max in one race. Three rounds ago, after Italy, Louise was 183 points behind the leader. Three consecutive wins have reduced that gap to 52.

Max still leads after sharing tenth in the round with Scott Harvey on 141 points. Fooch finishes twenty-first and remains fourth overall, still four championship positions higher than last year's eighth-place finish, but now 143 points behind the tied pair in second.

The midfield stories keep building. Martin West finishes fourth, continuing a remarkable sequence of twelfth in Italy, tenth in Madrid, seventh in Baku and now fourth in Bahrain. Defending champion Phil Motteram takes fifth, giving him three consecutive top-five finishes after second in Madrid and third in Baku.

Four competitors share the biggest rise at two places each: Matt Magraith moves into ninth, Scott Harvey climbs to twelfth, Adolfo Fernández Benito reaches thirteenth, and Alan Thomas rises to thirty-seventh after finishing sixth in the round. Jon Evans, Michael Sideris and Corey McDonald share the biggest fall at two places. Richard Saing, third in last year's championship, finishes thirty-eighth and slips to eleventh overall.

Sixteen rounds complete. Max still leads, but Louise has turned a 183-point deficit into 52 across three races, JR is back on the podium, the defending champion keeps climbing, and the battle for second is now a dead heat.

Next stop, Singapore.
